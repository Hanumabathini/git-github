1. Summary

Bottom line Start now on the laptop you already have (RTX 5070 8 GB, WanGP installed and working). It costs nothing extra. Build an audience for about 3 months. Only buy a desktop once growth is proven, and if possible after GPU and memory prices come down.
  • Hardware prices are unusually high right now. As of November 2026 the RTX 5070 Ti sells for about $1,340 (launch price $749), the RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB for about $780–805 (launch $429), and 32 GB DDR5 memory kits for roughly $250–370, about 3–4× last year's price. The $2,000 "Basic Plus" build discussed earlier no longer fits $2,000. See Section 3 for builds that do.
  • YouTube ad revenue today requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 10 million Shorts views (90 days). From 1 February 2027 this becomes 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views for new applicants (subscribers stay at 1,000). Channels already accepted are not affected, so qualifying before February 2027 is a real advantage.
  • AI video is allowed and can be monetized, but YouTube refuses to pay for "generic or repetitive" mass-produced content (rules clarified July 2026). Original research, script and point of view are what get approved.
  • Realistic AI footage must be disclosed with YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" setting. Disclosure itself does not reduce reach or earnings.

3.3 Ways to spend less

  • Wait for prices to settle. The GPU and memory surge in 2026 was steep and fast: the 5070 Ti rose $160 in eight days in November. Watch Black Friday (27 Nov 2026) and early-2027 prices, but don't count on bargains.
  • Used RTX 3090 24 GB: 24 GB of VRAM is excellent for WanGP. Buy only from a seller with returns, and test with nvidia-smi plus a WanGP render.
  • Reuse what you have: keep rendering on the laptop overnight. It's slower, but already paid for.
  • Don't buy: GPUs with 8–12 GB of VRAM, AMD cards for WanGP (supported, but slower and less tested), or cheap unbranded power supplies.

Also required: live in a country where YPP is available, follow YouTube's monetization policies, have no active Community Guidelines strike, 2-step verification on your Google account, and a linked AdSense account (you must be 18+, or use a parent's/guardian's AdSense account).

Important detailViews of Shorts in the Shorts feed do not count toward the 4,000 (or 8,000) watch hours. Only long-form watch time counts. Shorts help you gain subscribers; the long documentaries earn the watch hours.

4.2 What you would earn

  • Long videos: creators receive 55% of ad revenue on their long-form videos. Tech, AI and finance topics usually earn more per view than entertainment.
  • Shorts: paid from a shared Shorts revenue pool, usually much less per view than long videos.
  • Per-view earnings vary hugely by country of the viewers, topic and season, so don't plan your finances on a fixed number.
  • Many creators earn more from sponsorships and affiliate links than from ads once they have a loyal audience.

5. AI-content rules (most important for your channel)

5.1 "Generic or repetitive" content is not monetized

In July 2026 YouTube clarified its monetization guidelines (the section formerly called "inauthentic content"). Three types are not paid:

  1. Generic or repetitive content: mass-produced videos, near-identical templates, and tutorials that just copy what already exists.
  2. Unsatisfying or off-putting content: low-effort "AI slop" that wastes viewers' time.
  3. AI personas discussing sensitive topics: for example, a synthetic presenter giving news, health or finance claims.

YouTube states that using AI for scripting, editing or production is fine if the video offers unique value. The penalty is usually losing monetization for the whole channel, not just one video, so this matters a lot.

How your channel stays on the right side
  • Every video has its own researched script with facts, sources and your analysis (like the fact-checked Terafab script).
  • Give a clear point of view: explain why it matters, what's uncertain, and what you think.
  • Vary format and visuals. Don't publish dozens of near-identical template videos.
  • Consider your own voice for narration, at least sometimes, and a consistent channel identity.
  • Quality over quantity: 2–3 strong videos a week beats 20 weak ones.

5.2 Disclose realistic AI footage

  • When uploading, answer "Altered or synthetic content: Yes" in the Details section for realistic AI scenes (factories, people, places, events that didn't happen as shown).
  • The label appears in the description (sometimes on the player). Disclosing does not reduce reach or earnings. Not disclosing can lead to a label being added for you, and repeated failure to disclose can bring penalties.
  • Also label inside the video: "CONCEPT VISUALIZATION", "AI-GENERATED VISUALIZATION" (your scripts already do this).

5.3 Real people, brands and misleading content

  • Never generate a real person's face or voice (for example Elon Musk) saying or doing things. It risks privacy and impersonation removal, strikes and legal problems.
  • Don't use company logos (Tesla, SpaceX) in a way that suggests the company made or endorses your video. Mentioning names in titles for news and commentary is normal.
  • Don't present AI footage as real footage, and don't present plans or targets as achievements (e.g., "Terafab is producing chips" would be false).
  • Thumbnails and titles must match the video. Misleading thumbnails can break spam and deceptive-practices policies.

5.4 Copyright

  • Use only music, sound effects, voices, images and footage you created or have a licence for. Your current music, effects and narration are original or Apache-2.0.
  • YouTube's Content ID automatically detects copyrighted music; one match can send a video's earnings to the rights holder. Three copyright strikes terminate a channel.
  • The YouTube Audio Library offers free tracks that are safe to monetize.
  • Check the licence of every AI model you use. Wan2.2 (Apache-2.0) and Kokoro (Apache-2.0) allow commercial use; verify any new model or LoRA before using it in monetized videos.

7. Money, tax and legal basics

  • Payments: YouTube pays through AdSense once you pass the payment threshold (commonly $100 or the local equivalent), after identity, address and bank verification.
  • US tax form: every YouTube creator must submit US tax information in AdSense. Creators outside the US usually submit a W-8BEN. Without it, Google can withhold up to 24% of total earnings. With it, only US-viewer earnings are withheld, at 0–30% depending on your country's tax treaty.
  • Local income tax: channel income is taxable where you live. Keep records of income and expenses (GPU, PC, software and electricity may be deductible business expenses, depending on local law). Ask a local tax adviser once income starts.
  • Sponsorships: always tick YouTube's "includes paid promotion" box and say it clearly in the video. Advertising rules in most countries (for example, the FTC in the US or ASCI in India) require clear disclosure.
  • Contracts: read brand-deal terms for usage rights, exclusivity and payment dates before signing.

9. Scripting guide: writing each video

The script is what makes a video original. It's your main protection against YouTube's "generic or repetitive" rule, and the main reason viewers stay. Spend more time on the script than on rendering.

9.1 Script workflow (per video, about 2–4 hours)

StepWhat to doOutput
1. Topic checkIs it searched or trending (YouTube search suggestions, Google Trends)? Can you add something others haven't: newer facts, a clearer explanation, a strong opinion?One-sentence angle
2. ResearchCollect 3–5 reliable sources (official announcements, Reuters/AP, company filings, respected trade press). Note dates. Separate facts, plans/targets and opinions.Source list with dates
3. OutlineHook → context → 4–8 key beats → "what's uncertain" → your conclusion → question for comments.Beat list
4. Write narrationAbout 2.3–2.6 words per second (140–155 words per minute). A 10-second scene holds about 22–25 words; a 3-minute video about 420–460 words.Narration text per scene
5. Fact-check passEvery number, date and name must match a source. Use cautious wording for plans ("targets", "proposed", "aims to").Checked script + sources in description
6. Visual promptsFor each scene, write a motion prompt: subject movement, camera movement, lighting, style. No text inside the video.Prompt per 5-second shot
7. LabelsMark which scenes need "CONCEPT VISUALIZATION" or similar overlays.Overlay list
8. Read aloudRead the whole script at speaking speed. Cut anything that stumbles or exceeds the scene time.Final script

9.6 Titles, descriptions and thumbnails

  • Title: 50–70 characters, with the main keyword first. Example: "TERAFAB: Elon Musk's AI Chip Factory Explained".
  • Description: 2–3 summary lines, the AI disclosure sentence, chapters (0:00 …), sources, then 3–5 hashtags.
  • Thumbnail: one strong image, 2–3 words of large text, high contrast, labelled "CONCEPT" if AI-made. No fake real-person photos.

Making your next video with the same pipeline

  1. Write the script with the 9.5 scene cards.
  2. Copy the terafab folder to a new name. In scripts\build_manifest.py, replace the scene list (title, narration, sound, label, two prompts per scene).
  3. python scripts\build_manifest.py, then python scripts\make_audio.py --model-dir <folder>.
  4. python scripts\render_scenes.py --start-rung 0 (overnight), then python scripts\assemble_movie.py.
  5. Check output\render_report.md, then upload using the checklist in Section 8.
TipAsk Claude to draft a new build_manifest.py scene list from your script. Then review and fact-check it yourself before rendering.
